# Consolidation — Explain, apply and evaluate

## A connected view of the term

This term began with AI decisions and ended with creating a small web resource. The connection is the responsible use of information systems. An AI service needs appropriate data, evaluation and human oversight. A network service needs connectivity, compatible equipment, controlled access and reliable operation. An online public service needs usable design and trustworthy handling of information. A learning resource needs explanations and meaningful activities, not simply a screen full of facts.

Keep the main classifications separate. **Coverage** describes geographical extent; **architecture** describes service roles; **topology** describes connections; **duplex mode** describes permitted directions; **medium** describes how a signal travels; and **protocol** describes communication rules. One installation can have a campus coverage, client/server file services, a tree of switched stars, full-duplex Ethernet endpoints, copper and fibre media, and TCP/IP communication. These descriptions complement one another.

## Extended practice questions

These are original classroom questions, not copied GCE questions or an official prediction of an examination. Attempt them before reading the guidance.

1. A school wants AI to decide who receives financial assistance. Explain the required data, an honest test, two ethical risks and a responsible decision process.
2. A college has a laboratory and library on one campus and a study centre in another town. Recommend coverage, service architecture and a physical layout. Explain one failure and one growth issue.
3. Two computers have link lights, but neither can open the other's shared folder. Describe a safe diagnosis that distinguishes addressing, service availability, authentication and permissions.
4. A teacher needs remote access to restricted files. Explain how a VPN, signed certificates, firewall policy and file permissions contribute different protections. Identify two remaining risks.
5. A registration record contains the wrong but in-range mark. Explain how validation and verification address different parts of the problem and how an authorised correction should be made.
6. Compare serial with parallel, synchronous with asynchronous, and analog with digital transmission. Explain why these are not three competing labels for one property.
7. A school proposes a standard copper Ethernet channel across 300 m between buildings. Evaluate fibre and directional wireless alternatives using the route, environment, cost, maintenance and resilience.
8. Describe a web request using DNS, transport, IP, local frames and physical signals. Explain where the OSI model helps and why not every actual protocol has an undisputed single-layer label.
9. Add even and odd parity to `1001001`. Show a single-bit error and explain why some two-bit errors pass. Describe how retransmission can help recover data.
10. Plan an online group assignment for learners with uneven connectivity. Include source evaluation, permissions, safe attachment sending, a controlled meeting and an asynchronous alternative.
11. Compare e-commerce, e-health and e-government for three community needs. Evaluate the benefits and barriers for users rather than only the service provider.
12. Build a two-page HTML resource for the fictional school. Explain the document structure, test the relative paths and navigation, and submit the complete folder with a debugging record.

## Answer planning and selected model reasoning

For Question 1, begin with a clearly limited purpose and relevant, permitted data. Avoid treating previous decisions as automatically fair labels. Separate training from independent testing and assess results for affected groups. Explain privacy and unfair exclusion, then connect safeguards to those harms. Keep accountable humans in the decision and provide an appeal route. A list of ethical words without actions is not a complete response.

For Question 2, distinguish the local or campus arrangement from the inter-town WAN. Explain why centrally managed files and accounts may suit the college, while recognising cost and shared-service dependence. A switched star within rooms and a hierarchical campus arrangement may be practical. Analyse an actual failed component and reserve justified growth capacity. Another technically sound design can be defended if it meets the needs.

For Question 3, preserve the approved configuration and test one hypothesis at a time. Check the physical interface and addresses, then a permitted reachability test, then the actual share name and service, then the account and effective permissions. Do not disable the firewall or grant anonymous full control to produce an apparent success. Record what each test establishes and what it leaves uncertain.

For Question 4, the VPN protects selected traffic between its endpoints. Signed certificates support trusted identity checks, firewall rules limit allowed traffic, and permissions limit resource operations. An infected endpoint or stolen authorised account remains a risk. Neither a tunnel nor a protected-connection icon proves that every action at the endpoint is safe.

For Question 9, the data has three 1s. Append 1 for even parity or 0 for odd parity under the stated right-appended convention. Explain the total count and show how an odd number of flips changes parity while an even number may not. A mismatch detects a problem but does not locate it. ARQ adds feedback, timeout and retransmission; sequence information helps manage repeats.

For Question 12, assess structure and behaviour. The tab title belongs in the head, presented content in the body, and links must point to actual files or existing fragment identifiers. A meaningful image needs appropriate alternative text. Open both pages locally, test navigation and resources, save and reload an edit, and correct a recorded fault. A screenshot of one attractive page is not proof that the whole folder works.

## Practical completion record

A learner should eventually be able to show the following on approved equipment, not only describe them. Keep evidence free of credentials and personal information.

- A simple Teachable Machine model, independent test observations and an explanation of at least one failure.
- A controlled two-computer share with configuration, service-access and permission-test evidence.
- An authenticated VPN connection to a prepared lab gateway, a permitted resource test and a teacher-designated blocked-resource observation; otherwise record the practical as still outstanding.
- A test of read-only and editing roles plus validation boundaries and a verification comparison on fictional data.
- Browser navigation and settings use, an approved e-mail account with confirmed attachment receipt, and a focused information search with source evaluation.
- A shared document with deliberate permissions and contribution evidence, plus participation in a controlled online meeting.
- A non-transactional inspection of an approved public-service page and a learning resource chosen for an actual access need.
- Two connected HTML pages, their local image, successful navigation tests and a short debugging record.

## Compact vocabulary reference

This reference helps you locate meanings already explained in the lessons. It is not a replacement for those explanations.

**AI:** computing concerned with tasks associated with intelligence. **Machine learning:** learning useful patterns from data. **Model:** the learned representation used for prediction. **Training:** adjusting a model from examples. **Inference:** using a trained model on input. **Generalisation:** useful performance on new cases. **Bias:** a systematic tendency that can distort results or disadvantage users.

**Network:** connected devices communicating through agreed rules. **Protocol:** communication rules. **LAN:** local-area connectivity. **CAN:** campus-area connectivity in this course. **WAN:** wide-area connectivity. **Client:** a service requester. **Server:** a service provider. **Topology:** a connection arrangement. **Switch:** a device forwarding local frames. **Router:** a device forwarding packets between IP networks.

**Throughput:** achieved useful transfer rate. **Latency:** delay. **Buffer:** temporary holding storage during transfer. **Interrupt:** a request for processor attention. **Polling:** repeated status checking. **Handshaking:** exchange of control information to coordinate communication. **Encapsulation:** adding relevant enclosing communication information. **Parity:** an agreed odd/even count relationship used for limited error detection.

**Authentication:** verifying identity. **Authorisation:** deciding permitted actions. **Confidentiality:** limiting information to authorised parties. **Integrity:** protecting against inappropriate alteration. **Availability:** access when needed. **Encryption:** transforming readable data into protected ciphertext using a key. **Digital signature:** cryptographic evidence supporting authenticity and integrity under a trusted key association. **Least privilege:** only the necessary authority.

**Validation:** checking input against defined rules. **Verification:** checking faithful copying against a source in this data-entry context. **Intranet:** internal organisational services using Internet technologies. **Extranet:** selected services extended to authorised external users. **VPN:** a protected logical connection over another network. **URL:** a resource location and access description. **Cookie:** browser-held site state used under applicable rules.

**CAL:** computer-assisted learning. **LMS:** a system organising learning resources and activities. **Synchronous learning:** same-time interaction. **Asynchronous learning:** participation at different times. **HTML:** markup describing web content structure. **Tag:** a markup token. **Element:** the structured unit including markup and content where applicable. **Attribute:** additional information on an element.

## Editorial method

The earlier Upper Sixth ICT file was reviewed for scope, lesson sequence and instructional limitations. This revision replaces table-dominated summaries with connected explanations, worked reasoning, original labelled diagrams, practical procedures and checks with answer guidance. Tables are discussed where they are genuine subject content, such as HTML data tables; they are not used to contain the book's main teaching text. Diagram captions explain what to notice rather than leaving instructions to draw a figure later.

Technical wording distinguishes concepts often blurred in short summaries: telemedicine from AI, coverage from architecture and topology, half-duplex shared Ethernet from switched full-duplex Ethernet, validation from verification, signatures from encryption, and VPN protection from authorisation. Local organisations in scenarios are fictional unless an official public platform is explicitly identified and sourced. Practical interfaces may change, so the teacher should pre-test approved tools and adapt control names without changing the underlying learning objective.
